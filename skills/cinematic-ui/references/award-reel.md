# The award reel — what winning sites actually do

Twenty-one Awwwards winners, studied frame by frame (October 2026): two Sites of the Year, fifteen Sites of
the Month, four recent Sites of the Day. Each was shot on desktop and phone through its intro, its scroll
and its gates, and probed for type, colour and stack. This file is what those frames have in common, what
they deliberately do differently, and the pass that turns a directed treatment into one that can stand
next to them.

## What they share — in order of how much it separates them from a generated page

### 1. One subject, many states — and the states are written first
Every winner has a lead the eye follows from loader to footer, and it is *the same subject in different
states*, never a new picture per section. Lando's helmet: wireframe → textured → archive → worn. Terminal's
truck: photograph → particle scan → wireframe → grid. Heron's floor plan: walls → furniture → dimensions →
violation tags. Santioni's saint: ochre robe → product-blue robe after he drinks. Four to seven states,
each a different *shot* of the subject. The skill already asks for an arc of three; the winners run five.
**Write the state list before the shot list**; then each scene's job is to deliver one state.

### 2. The world is the interface
The metaphor does not stop at the hero. It reaches the chrome: Siena's ENTER, menu and now-showing widget
are perforated cinema tickets; Ponpon's CTA is a media-player pill whose progress bar is reading progress;
Heron's frame is a drafting sheet with ruler ticks and registration marks; Terminal's cards carry the
notches of its logo; Lando's archive cards share the race-ticket silhouette; Dropbox's 404 is its tiles
falling in a pile. A generated page themes the hero and then reverts to stock UI. **Name the world object,
then list where it appears: loader, cut, button, progress mark, cursor, menu, footer, 404.**

### 3. The loader is shot one
No spinners, no lone percentages. The loader draws the first image or states the concept: Igloo's hero as
a 1 px wireframe blueprint that the render resolves over; Oryzo's coaster drawn in bezier construction
lines; Heron's building drawn stroke by stroke under a scan line with a boot log; Renaissance's "The *ai*
Edition" growing "Ren…ssance" around the *ai*; Lando's monogram scaling into a mask onto the portrait;
Cartier's single brand sentence with a counter. Three sites took over six seconds to load, and the loader
made the wait part of the film. Gates are allowed when they are the product's verb (draw a zero, ENTER on
a ticket, an age gate set as a title card).

### 4. Every boundary is a cut, and the cut is made of the world
None of the twenty-one joins two sections with a fade-up. Cuts seen: a brand-shaped mask (Lando's
monogram, ERA's arch, Ponpon's mascot head); zoom through a detail of the current set into the next
(Cartier through the gap between bracelet links, Son Daven through a tunnel of bars, Igloo through a ring
portal); a material wipe (Santioni's woodcut strokes, Son Daven's barcode dissolve, Lama's pixel mosaic,
Renaissance's painting torn into noise); panels with large radii sliding over the last section
(MindMarket); a hard clip rising from the bottom under fixed copy (Floema); a pull-back that makes the
whole shot one cell of the next layout (Heron). **The outgoing frame frames the incoming one.**

### 5. One accent, spent like money
Bases are two or three tones. The loud colour covers 2–5 % of the frame and has a stated meaning: Terminal's
lime is "the machine sees this / act here"; Heron's orange is "violation"; Santioni's red marks caption
keywords; Odyssée's only saturated pixel is a red dot beside a live clock; Lando's lime is handwriting,
never a fill; Oryzo's rainbow glow appears on one beat only. Light grounds are as common as dark (Lando, ERA, Heron, Belgrade, Floema, Dropbox, Renaissance are
light): darkness is not what makes these cinematic.

### 6. Type: two roles, one switch, an extreme size
- **Two roles that never swap jobs:** display + text, display + mono, serif + grotesk, lettering + caption.
  A third family appears only as a costume (Renaissance's swash serif).
- **One switch device per brand:** a serif word inside a grotesk line (Lando), a script word crossing
  condensed didone caps (ERA), big/small word pairs (Why Zero), bracketed mono labels (Lama, Heron),
  italic for "selected" (Belgrade). Exactly one, used everywhere.
- **Display size sits at an extreme.** Huge — 11–14 vw, up to a single word 94 % of the frame (Oryzo,
  Zentry, ERA, Belgrade) — or deliberately small, 2.4–4 vw, when the image carries the scale (Heron,
  Odyssée, Floema, Cartier). None of the twenty-one uses a centred mid-size 48–72 px H1 — a tendency, not a law: CSSDA winners
  Poly (52 px, centred) and Farm Minerals (54 px) won with one, because the scene carried the scale.
- **Numbers measured:** display line-height 0.8–0.9; tracking −0.02 to −0.06 em (Son Daven −0.064 em at
  144 px); labels 9–12 px tracked caps or mono at +0.06–0.14 em; display:label ratio 7:1 to 17:1.
- **Headlines of two to four words, punctuation as the punchline:** "No more chaos." "Complexity,
  delegated." "Isn't just a coaster."

### 7. Instruments, not navigation
The fixed chrome is three to five marks, and they report the film: ERA's hairline counter 00 → 41; Floema's
hairline at exactly half the viewport height filling with progress; Renaissance's I–XII chapter rail;
Cartier's seven-dot rail; Belgrade's lime hairline with 01–03; Odyssée's "3 : 4"; Why Zero's ruler counting
down to zero with an XP badge; Lama's live ticking clock and a nav caption rewritten per section ("CASES WITH
TEETH"). Corners carry jobs: identity top-left, action top-right or bottom-centre, status bottom-left.
A sound toggle appears wherever there is sound, off by default.

### 8. Captions as texture
Small precise metadata makes a frame read as an archive or a drawing set rather than a template: place and
year under photographs ("BARCELONA, 2024"), coordinates and indices in mono ("[831,0]", "[01]"),
"PORTFOLIO_CO_01 / TEMP 35.53" on a leader line, "( + )" toggles, "✦✦✦ LABEL ✦✦✦" eyebrows. They must be
true to the product and in the world's voice — a drafting sheet's labels on an architecture tool, ticket
stubs on a film foundation.

### 9. Type and image share depth
The lead passes in front of a word, the word sits between two planes: Oryzo's coaster crosses a headline
94 % wide; Renaissance's 194 px chapter word sits between a painted figure and a 3D prop; MindMarket's
ribbon wraps behind one person and in front of another; Why Zero's word pairs stand inside the 3D scene.

### 10. Spectacle is rationed
About two-thirds of the Renaissance scroll is a calm parchment feature list with "Read help doc" links.
Terminal carries a calculator, demo, forms and an FAQ — after a hero with no text at all. Son Daven
alternates dense and empty frames. The set pieces earn their weight because the document between them is
quiet and readable.

### 11. The first frame often has no headline
Belgrade opens on a pencil elevation; Odyssée on footage with an 18 px chrome band across the middle;
Santioni on mist; Igloo on a 25-word sentence in the corner; Terminal on its truck. The image is the
opening line, and the words arrive on the second beat. On a page that must convert on the first visit
(a demo request, pricing, an emergency number), the image-only beat is the loader, not the hero: the
headline and the primary action are in the DOM from first paint and on screen within about a second.

### 12. The audience performs the product's verb
The strongest interactions make the visitor do what the product does: draw a zero to enter a university
called Zero; drag a scanner over a plan to find code violations (Heron); hold and move to reach through
the portal as the saint does (Santioni); drive the portfolio (Bruno). Soft versions — a cursor chip that
says "WORK ( + )", hover that inverts a tile to black like selecting a layer — still belong to the world.

### 13. Every surface has a finish
Paper grain, leaf shadow, hatching, real pores, cellophane shine on a record sleeve, halftone on footage.
Flat fills appear by intent (Zentry's lavender, Dropbox's tiles), never by default.

### 14. The phone gets its own cut
Most recompose for portrait rather than squeezing (Grids won CSSDA's Website of the Day while telling
phones it was "designed for desktop" — the exception that proves the cost): Son Daven moves its wordmark into
the forest; Renaissance puts its index inside the loader's golden rectangle; Belgrade adds beats; Ponpon
drops the mask; Siena re-stacks its poster.

## Where they differ — choose, do not average

| Dial | Range seen | Choose by |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | One WebGL canvas with virtual scroll (Igloo, Santioni, Cartier) · DOM + GSAP + smooth scroll + video (Lando, MindMarket, Lama) · no WebGL at all (ERA, Odyssée, Dropbox Brand) | The medium. ERA, Odyssée and Dropbox Brand draw no WebGL at all; the award comes from direction, not WebGL |
| Scroll model | Long document (13–56k px) · a fixed stage where scroll is a playhead and the page never moves (Igloo, Cartier, Siena, Ponpon, Santioni) | How much reading the product needs |
| Participation | A gate that must be performed · one soft interaction · scroll only | Whether the product has a verb a hand can do |
| Copy load | One sentence (Igloo) → polemic (Why Zero) → sales detail (ERA, Terminal) | The brief; keep every word the client supplied |
| Palette regime | Fixed · ground tweened by scroll (Lando olive → grey → off-white) · regrade per act (Why Zero) · colour per chapter or category (Ponpon, Floema, Cartier) | Whether colour carries the argument |
| Type scale | Monumental or deliberately small | Whether type or image carries the scale |

## The second jury — CSS Design Awards

Fifteen CSSDA winners (Websites of the Month and Day, one Special Kudos, 2024–2026: Farm Minerals, 100 Lost
Species, ComPsych Brand Hub, Lightweight, Exat Typeface, Contra Cost Calculator, D&G Gift Finder, Grids,
A24, Agrumea, Aardvark Book Club, Quaker School, Max Mara Jacket Circle, Poly, Mejuma Tuscany) were studied
the same way. CSSDA scores **UI, UX and Innovation separately and averages them**, so UX is a full third of
the mark — and its winners show it. What they add to the fourteen patterns above:

1. **The conversion tool is the set piece.** Contra's cost calculator runs on the screen of a 3D handheld;
   D&G's gift quiz is a chat whose answers grow richer each step; Poly's search field is typed by scroll.
   When a form, quiz, calculator or search *is* the promise, stage it as the climax — on a world object,
   one question per shot, every answer echoed back — with real inputs, labels and keyboard paths.
2. **The lead's states come from the use cycle.** Farm Minerals' tablet drops into water and dissolves;
   Aardvark's box opens until the camera is inside the lid; Lightweight's wheel goes whole → section →
   exploded → whole; Poly's messy desk sorts itself. For a product: packed → applied → working → result.
   The how-it-works section is then the set piece, not a feature grid.
3. **Cards carry the buyer's decision data.** Mejuma's ruled Price / Location / Size rows; Agrumea's
   allergens in bold and weights as chips; Farm Minerals' "Source: FAO, 2023" and "Next update". Every
   product or listing card shows the two to four facts people compare on, and every number has a source
   or a date. Captions-as-texture (pattern 8) is the decoration; this is the job.
4. **Wayfinding stays, and is designed.** Quaker's search suggests "Crisis intervention"; A24 has "Index
   12" and a bottom pill nav on the phone; Poly a bottom dock; D&G puts Back and Restart in the quiz header.
   Instruments add to navigation; they never replace it. On phones, navigation lives in the thumb zone
   (the pill, the 目次 rail and the thumb bar: `production-design.md`, *The interface inside the film*).
5. **Accessibility and consent are visible craft.** Skip links, visible focus rings, "Reject all" the
   same size as "Accept all", sound announced as optional and off — the UX PROOF line of the award pass.
6. **Hover shows the hidden side.** Agrumea's jar turns to its nutrition label; an Exat glyph opens with
   its cap-height and baseline marked. The best hover is the side a shopper would turn the object to see.
7. **The loader can sit inside the scene.** Poly's laptop reads "Loading experience…" while the page is
   already readable; 100 Lost Species turns its "100" counter into the Enter button.
8. **A collection gets lenses.** A wall of many items offers two or three sort lenses (100 Lost Species:
   by visuals, by name, by date of extinction). A countdown is earned only when time is the subject, and
   it ends on the call to action.

They also correct the fourteen: the world reaching the chrome (§2) is a lift, not a requirement — 100 Lost
Species and ComPsych won with plain pills and stock arrows; palettes can be many flat colours when colour is
the system (Aardvark, Quaker, Exat); a plain hairline loader can win (Lightweight); and when the set piece
*is* the explanation (Farm Minerals), it does not need rationing.

## The lazy reading of these sites — refuse it

Award sites are easy to imitate badly. The imitation keeps the surface and loses the mechanism:

- A preloader with a percentage counter and a logo, unrelated to the first shot
- Giant type at 12 vw on every section, so nothing is large
- A lime or orange accent on every button, link and heading — an accent with no meaning
- Mono labels and "[01]" brackets sprinkled on a product that is not about precision
- A custom cursor blob, magnetic buttons and a marquee, because winners "have them"
- A themed hero followed by stock cards: the world never reaches the chrome
- Clip-path wipes between every section regardless of what the sections are
- A different picture per section: no lead, no states
- Copying a winner's signature move unchanged (the ticket buttons, the barcode dissolve). Study the
  mechanism, then find this product's own object: a winner's move is its costume. The test: **if the
  object is the product's own working tool or material** (a lab's contact sheet, an architect's drawing
  set, a watchmaker's bracelet), it is native, even when a winner used something near it; if it only
  evokes the product's mood, it is costume
- The same chrome kit on every production — a rail, a counter, a giant numeral, a red circle. Instruments
  rotate like directors do: the filmography logs them

## The award pass — fill this in the treatment before the shot list

One block, twelve lines. It does not replace the casting session; it is how the director's mechanisms reach
every frame.

```
WORLD OBJECT   the one thing from the subject's own craft the whole site is made of (drafting sheet, ticket, record sleeve, grid, arch)
LEAD STATES    5–7 states of one subject, in order, each a shot (state → scene)
LOADER         shot one: how the loader draws the first image or states the concept; what it becomes
CUT MATERIAL   what boundaries are made of (a brand shape mask, a zoom through a detail, a material wipe…) and the one exception
ACCENT         one colour, its meaning in five words, budget ≤ 5 % of the frame; say why the primary CTA belongs to that meaning, or give the CTA its own treatment
TYPE           two roles · the one switch device · display at which extreme (≥ 11 vw or ≤ 4 vw) and why
INSTRUMENTS    3–5 fixed marks and what each reports (progress in the world's language, a counter, a clock, sound)
VERB           what the visitor's hand does that the product does
TRIGGERS       which beats are scrubbed, which play on entry, which autoplay, which wait for a click, hover or drag (at least four kinds)
TYPE CAST      the signature type move + the type department: effect · job · trigger (`references/type-motion.md`)
UX PROOF       skip link · visible focus · decline as easy as accept · sound announced and off · Back/Restart in multi-step flows · search/index where there is a collection
RHYTHM         which scenes are set pieces and which are calm documents; the phone's own cut in one line
```

Then check it the way a jury would. Awwwards scores **design 40 %, usability 30 %, creativity 20 %,
content 10 %**: usability is nearly a third of the mark, so the calm documents, the readable copy, the
reduced-motion cut and the phone cut are not concessions — they are score. In the screening, add three
questions to the director's notes:

1. **Squint at the contact sheet.** Is the same subject in most frames, visibly in a different state at
   the end? Is the accent rare enough to find in one glance?
2. **Look at the chrome.** Would the buttons, progress mark and loader be recognisable as *this* world
   with the hero cropped out?
3. **Look at every boundary** (`--cuts`). Is any cut a plain fade or a fade-up? Is each cut made of the
   world?

## Type motion in the winners

Each winner uses **one** type move, matched to its medium, and repeats it: Siena's slit-scan roll (the
reel); Cartier's letters arriving in the 3D room while the next title ghosts in; Terminal's paragraphs
filling grey-to-ink as reading progress; Son Daven's quote lit character by character with scroll;
Belgrade's words resolving from blur; Oryzo's giant word shrinking to dock as the next section's label;
Dropbox's selection boxes around words; Ponpon's marquee inside the player pill. The generic split-line
fade-up appears on none of them as the signature. The kit's type library (`kit/type-fx.js`,
`references/type-motion.md`) has forty-four moves to cast from, four of them (slit-scan, read-fill, blur-resolve, select-box) built from these winners; cast one or two, by the beat.

## Screening WebGL reference sites

To study a reference site with `scripts/screening.mjs`, run it `--headed` on a real GPU: headless Chrome
blanked the canvas of two winners. Pass gates by their exact label; a loose match on a word like
"Explore" clicks a product link and shoots the wrong page.
