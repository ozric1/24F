# Art direction — the film's world, and its poster

The grade (`grades.md`) says what the light is made of. Art direction says what the *world* is made of: its
shapes, materials, ornament, architecture, how full the rooms are, and the graphic design inside it (signs,
documents, title cards). A site that has the right grade but generic rounded cards, pill buttons and a
four-column footer has the film's light falling on a template.

Art direction is easier to fake than colour, because the film's props are right there to copy. **Borrow the
production designer's mechanism, never the props.** A Wes Anderson site may be built as a frontal, symmetric
dollhouse cross-section with hand-lettered document cards. It may not have a pink hotel, a Mendl's box or a
lobby-boy hat.

The poster is a third, separate language (*The poster layer*, below).

Research the cast film live (`film-research.md`). The fourteen films at the end are worked examples of the
result, not a menu.

## The ART block

```
ART          film · production designer (+ art director, set decorator, graphic designer) · sources [P]/[S]/[I]
SHAPE        the world's geometry: corridor and doorway frames · frontal symmetry · accreted layers · brutalist slab · triangle and mirror
MATERIAL     surfaces and wear: wallpaper · black marble · concrete and water · Bakelite and chrome · plywood · photocopy and tape
ORNAMENT     pattern language, and where it may live (one scene's ground, the surround, a divider), never behind text
ERA          architecture and period, and which era of *making* the type is cast from (in-world lettering genre, not the film's logo)
DENSITY      sparse ↔ dense, per scene: which scenes are dressed, which are empty
GRAPHICS     the in-world graphic design: signage as texture · hand-lettered documents · two UI dialects · none
UI MAP       frame · grid · panels and cards · buttons · dividers · forms · footer · imagery: what each becomes in this world
COSTUME      the props, logos and iconography a lazy imitation would paste on: refused
POSTER       the composition grammar borrowed for the first frame, OG image, title cards and credits (see below)
```

Write the UI MAP line by line, because that is where the art direction reaches the page. A grade covers the
pictures. The UI MAP covers everything else. If a button or footer could belong to any site, that line is
unwritten.

## The dials

- **Shape.** Where does the world put its edges? Wong Kar-wai and William Chang see characters "always" through
  doors, windows and corridors, so the page frames content through nested frames. Anderson and Stockhausen build
  frontal cross-sections on a central axis. Blade Runner's city is accretion: Mead's pipes and units added onto
  existing facades. 2049's one-word brief was "Brutality". Neon Demon's door is a triangle of mirrors.
- **Material.** What the surfaces are and how worn they are: Demy's custom-printed wallpaper, Tyrell's black marble,
  Deckard's Bakelite kitchen, 2049's concrete box around water, Mid90s' photocopies, stickers and tape. Material
  sets the button, the card and the divider: a Bakelite button, a slab with one lit edge, a sticker that straightens
  on hover.
- **Ornament.** Pattern is a decision about *where*. Demy puts it on every wall and matches the costume to it.
  Flowers of Shanghai keeps it in the surround so the faces read calm. 2049 has almost none. On a page, ornament
  lives in one scene's ground, a border or a divider, and never behind text.
- **Era of making.** Cast type from the lettering genre of the film's world and period, never from its logo:
  early-1960s Hong Kong commercial lettering, 1930s Central European signpainting, 1910s handbills, late-Qing book
  type, post-war institutional wayfinding, 1980s geometric computer display.
- **Density.** How full each room is, per scene. Blade Runner alternates a sparse monolith office and a dense
  street. Mid90s sets a bland, sparse house against a crowded skate-shop wall. Grand Budapest marks its eras by
  density. Write density per scene in the shot list, and make the contrast between scenes the device.
- **In-world graphics.** Annie Atkins hand-lettered every document of a fictional state. Territory Studio gave 2049
  two UI dialects, polished for the powerful and degraded for the working. Blade Runner's signage reads as texture,
  not as text to read. A site can have two dialects too: a polished marketing layer and a worn working layer.

## The poster layer

A film's poster is usually made by a different designer or agency from the film's art department, and it says
something the frames do not. The Godfather's black emblem names the power structure; the film shows family
ceremonies. In the Mood for Love's Japanese sheet promises an intimacy the film withholds. Grand Budapest's
poster is a roster of faces in key cubbies, while the film is mostly two people in symmetry.

**Borrow the composition grammar, never the poster.** Take the subject placement, the scale, the area of silence,
how the type sits in the image, the crop, the print process and the colour count. Never take the artwork, the
lettering, the tagline or the billing block's wording. That would be costume and a copyright problem.

Look at the poster to read it (`film-research.md`). Do not store or embed it. Text sources rarely describe a
poster's layout, so any composition you did not see is inference.

**Grammars to cast** (one per production). These are common ones. If the cast film's own poster has another
grammar, name it and use that:

| Grammar | Mechanism | The cheap imitation |
|---|---|---|
| Reductive symbol (Saul Bass) | One symbol from a few flat, hand-cut shapes; one or two inks; it summarises the film's idea instead of showing a scene | A smooth vector silhouette in retro orange-teal |
| Fused metaphor (Polish Poster School) | One painterly or collage metaphor; letters drawn into the image, not set beside it | A handwriting font next to stock art |
| Front and back (Japanese chirashi) | One image-led front; a dense information verso (staff, cast, stills) | Scattered Japanese characters as decoration |
| Painted stack (Hong Kong 80s–90s, Struzan, Alvin) | One painted surface holding faces and incidents at several scales under one light | A "painted" filter on a photo collage |
| Grid and type as image (Swiss / International) | The grid sets the position of one element; type is the picture | A grotesque on white with a decorative grid overlay |
| Commissioned concept (Criterion) | A designer reads the film and makes one concept; stills reworked, not dropped in | A bordered still with a number in the corner |
| Ink budget (Mondo screen print) | A hard cap of 3–6 flat inks, regular and variant colourways | Grain over a digital collage |
| Roster in the world's object (Grand Budapest) | The faces sit in an object from the film's world (key cubbies), on a uniform grid | Floating heads |
| **Floating heads** (the lazy cut) | Stacked star faces over a gradient, title at the foot: every film flattened to one frame | (refused) |

**On the page:**
- **First frame.** Choose the grammar before the layout. The subject takes 35–60 % of the frame height. The
  rest is silence that stays empty until scroll. The title is part of the image (drawn into it, locked to the
  grid, or a small mass under the symbol). The CTA sits where a billing block would: small, below, the title's
  width. A poster has no "headline, subhead, two buttons".
- **OG image (1200×630).** A re-crop of the same grammar, not a screenshot: one subject and one title inside the
  central 1000×500. It must read at 300 px wide. Make one OG per page type with the same grid and a different
  subject.
- **Title cards and intertitles.** A Swiss or Bass poster reduced to type: one element, one position, held. The
  section cards are a series: same grid, different mass.
- **Footer as end credits.** Use a billing block: one narrow column, a strict size ladder, legal and sitemap in its
  smallest tier. Follow the chirashi rule: the front is image-led and the back is dense, and the two densities
  never share a band. The last frame is the poster again, reduced.
- **Phone.** A phone is a poster format. Recompose to the poster's native portrait: subject in the top 55–65 %
  of the small viewport height, title in the lower third, CTA under it. Vertical type only for CJK, with a real
  `writing-mode: vertical-rl`, never rotated Latin. The OG image is a crop of the same portrait master.

## Worked examples

Each entry is the result of researching one film: the sourced decisions (tags: [P] a maker quoted, [S] critics or
press, [W] wiki, credits only, [I] inference), the UI map, and the costume refused. The research notes behind them, with every URL, gap
and dropped claim, are kept with the skill's lab material. Treat [I] as a reading, not a fact.

### Retro / nostalgic

**R1 · Wong Kar-wai / William Chang — Chungking Express, Fallen Angels, In the Mood for Love.** Characters seen
"always" through doors, windows and corridors [S]. Cramped rooms, clutter on screen, an empty corridor as a
character [S]. Cheongsam fabrics that complement or clash with the décor [S]. Chungking used real locations; *In
the Mood for Love*'s 1962 Hong Kong was built in Bangkok [S]. In-world lettering: unsourced.
*UI map:* content in a door-width column inside nested frames, dark empty sides. Tall portrait cards. Dividers are
1px lines that stop short of the edge like a door frame. One wallpaper-like tile as the ground of one scene,
changed per scene as the dresses change. Images cropped by an occluder (frame, curtain, slats) over 20–35 %.
Type cast from early-1960s Hong Kong printed commercial lettering.
*Costume:* neon-sign graphics, the films' title lettering, cheongsam silhouettes as chrome, the star clock, the
thermos, skyline stock.

**R2 · Jacques Demy / Bernard Evein — The Umbrellas of Cherbourg.** Shot on location. Candy-painted shopfronts,
patterned wallpaper in every interior, costume matched to wall [S]. Most wallpapers specially printed (a cited
figure of about 90 %; attribution unclear).
*UI map:* each scene a flat room. One wall colour fills the frame. Cards take the ground's colour family,
not white. One 2–3-colour wallpaper tile per scene with type on a solid plate, plus a wallpaper border strip as a
divider. Square solid buttons. The footer is a shopfront awning band. Type cast from late-1950s French
hand-painted shop lettering.
*Costume:* umbrellas as icon, cursor or loader; the Cherbourg skyline; a pink fade.

**R3 · Coppola / Willis / Dean Tavoularis — The Godfather.** A family film, not a "cliché" gangster film [P/S].
Half-open blinds in Vito's office; top light pushing through the set [S]. The bright wedding is cut against the
dark office [S]. Pattern, density and graphics: unsourced.
*UI map:* a near-black room lit by one overhead radial behind the current element. Dark wood-brown plates with
a 1px bevel. A blinds-slat overlay on one edge of one hero, never across text. Matte, low-contrast buttons
whose label brightens on hover. One bright "wedding" scene among dark ones. Type cast from early-1970s American
formal print and engraved stationery.
*Costume:* the puppet-strings mark, roses, oranges, fedoras, Italian-script lettering.

**R4 · Hou Hsiao-hsien / Hwarng Wern-ying — Flowers of Shanghai.** Built and shot entirely in a Taiwan studio,
interiors only [S]. Candles instead of bulbs (Ah Cheng's proposal) [W]. Faces "subtly oppose" the ornament and
primary hues of each room [S].
*UI map:* the viewport is one room, and moves between rooms are slow lateral slides. Ornament lives in the
surround, and the content area stays plain. Lacquer plates with a 2px border in one hue per house. A lattice band
only between rooms. Type cast from late-Qing book typography (Ming-style serif).
*Costume:* lanterns, brush calligraphy, chinoiserie borders, red paper-cuts.

**R5 · Terrence Malick / Jack Fisk — Days of Heaven.** A whole house built, not a facade, modelled on Hopper's
*House by the Railroad*. Fisk: "Hopper simplifies images, and that's what production design is" [S]. Window light
only by day [P].
*UI map:* full-width horizontal bands with one isolated vertical structure per scene. Content on the horizon line.
A card is a lit window in a dark interior, with mullions. The divider is a 1px horizon. Type cast from 1910s
handbills and timetables, sparsely set.
*Costume:* wheat-field stock, locusts, sepia, old-photo borders, the house silhouette as a logo.

**R6 · Wes Anderson / Adam Stockhausen / Annie Atkins — The Grand Budapest Hotel.** Interior built in a disused
Görlitz department store, palette from regional buildings, miniatures and painted backdrops [P/S]. 1930s lobby
rich, the 1960s "Eastern Bloc-ified" [P/S]. Atkins hand-lettered every graphic prop for a fictional state from
1930s Eastern European references, with a real typewriter and a dip pen [S].
*UI map:* strict central axis; stacked frontal rooms; the container's aspect ratio changes per chapter. Cards are
documents: letterhead, double rule, hand-lettered heading, a stamp. Luggage-tag buttons. Telegram-form fields.
Density marks the era: rich, then stripped. Type cast from 1930s Central European signpainting, typewriter and
dip pen.
*Costume:* the pink facade, the Mendl's box, the lobby-boy hat, Futura-style cards, filigree copied from the film.

**R7 · Jonah Hill / Jahmin Assa — Mid90s.** No "ode to the 90s": pre-1990s objects with a light sprinkling of
period pieces. A skate shop that "could close down the next week", its graphics made by whoever could draw best.
A bland, sparse ranch house before the dense shop [P].
*UI map:* quiet, even house scenes against shop scenes built as a wall of flyers, stickers and tear-outs
(unaligned, 1–3° rotations, tape corners). Sticker buttons that straighten on hover. Media in 4:3. Wear in the
objects, degradation in the grade, never both on one element. Type cast from hand-drawn marker and zine lettering.
*Costume:* VHS logo, scan lines, "REC", real skate-brand logos, Pogs, Walkman icons.

### Modern-future / neon

**N1 · Ridley Scott / Lawrence G. Paull / Syd Mead — Blade Runner.** "You wear your guts on the outside":
accretion, with pipes and units bolted onto existing facades [S]. Black-marble Tyrell office, Bakelite and
Moderne kitchen, "a messy patina" [S]. Signage works as texture, not text [S]. Street density like "Hong Kong or
Calcutta" [S].
*UI map:* plain slabs with service layers visibly attached (offset 1–2px, a hairline seam). Conduit dividers with
joints. Moulded ivory or black buttons with a chrome hairline. The densest type in the footer, the street level.
Hero sparse like the marble office, middle dense like the street. Type cast from 1970s–80s commercial signage
plus a 1940s Deco layer.
*Costume:* kanji signage, the pyramid, the eye, the Spinner, rain, the cyan grid.

**N2 · Denis Villeneuve / Dennis Gassner — Blade Runner 2049.** The brief was "Brutality" [S]. Stark Brutalist
architecture (Barbican, Trellick Tower), Wallace on a platform in water inside a concrete box, square
windowless walls carrying moving light [S]. No practical neon: "It was all LED" [S]. Territory Studio's screen
graphics, polished for Wallace and degraded for the LAPD and K [S].
*UI map:* heavy single-tone blocks with no radius, one lit slot or caustic per block. Wide empty planes. Dividers
by tone change. Only the active field is lit. Two UI dialects: polished for the hero, ghosted and worn for working
data. Type cast from post-war institutional and wayfinding sans, plus mono for data.
*Costume:* holograms, the giant statues, an orange-haze filter, neon glow (it was LED by design).

**N3 · Nicolas Winding Refn / Beth Mickle — Drive.** About 15 key sets. A sparse driver's flat, a warm wallpapered
one, a garage bay painted rich blue, an office rich green; a mirror-ring strip club with jewel carpet. No signage
added to the streets [S].
*UI map:* most of the page plain and undressed. Three or four fully dressed rooms as flat painted colour blocks.
One baroque moment (mirror ring). Quiet toile only behind the form. One period brush script for a single title.
*Costume:* the scorpion, the pink title script, the synthwave grid, toothpicks, gloves.

**N4 · Nicolas Winding Refn / Elliott Hostetter — The Neon Demon.** A triangle of mirrors as the runway door, a
mirror room like "an inverted pyramid", ribbon-LED outlines, gold paper, silver silk; Guy Bourdin as the reference
[S].
*UI map:* each section a stage with one reflective surface. Triangles as image masks and section entrances,
once each. One thin ribbon line per section, one colour each. Only the featured panel carries an edge. Type cast
from 1970s–80s fashion-editorial display, large and sparse.
*Costume:* the triangle as a logo, eye-jewel imagery, glitter, blood.

**N5 · Michael Mann / David Wasco — Collateral.** Thin: no designer interview found. About 30 electroluminescent
panels per cab, light with no visible source; a tower set made entirely of glass; a bar lit only by black light
[S].
*UI map (mostly [I]):* ordinary commercial structure, the effort in glass and reflection. Panels reflect
neighbouring light; nothing is outlined. One overloaded single-colour room as the exception. Type is unstylised
civic and commercial signage.
*Costume:* the cab and meter, tail-light bokeh, the grey suit, lens flares.

**N6 · Gaspar Noé / Marc Caro, Kikuo Ohta — Enter the Void.** Credits conflict between databases. Noé wanted
Tokyo to look like "TRON or BLADE RUNNER"; in-frame practicals and a programmed wash, blue avoided [S].
Materials and ornament: unsourced.
*UI map (mostly [I]):* one continuous dark space instead of separate pages. Small light sources at the edges and a
clear centre. A slow hue wash over all panels together. Light bleed instead of dividers.
*Costume:* POV hero video, sign clip-art, strobing text.

**N7 · Joseph Kosinski / Darren Gilford — Tron: Legacy.** "Light" as the key design element. The original's
geometry kept but "softened". The Grid "clean surfaces and lines" against the textured real world. Light built
into props and floors; hexagon light-film on suits [S].
*UI map:* dark clean planes with a small radius. Only the active control emits a lit seam. One under-lit floor
line as a divider. Warm, textured real-world sections against cool, clean Grid sections. Type cast from 1980s
geometric computer display, softened.
*Costume:* the disc, light-cycle trails, circuit wallpaper, a glowing outline on every card.
